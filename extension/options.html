Where prompts go

Where a prompt goes when you open a new tab. The pills above the search box change it too — they are the same setting, in the place you are most likely to want it.

What each option does

The first four go where they say, whatever your browser's default engine is. Auto and Default engine only hand the query to whatever engine Chrome is set to instead — so they follow your browser, and they route to ChatGPT for anyone using OpenAI's own search extension. Archer never changes that setting.

Weather

A small card in the corner of the new tab. Type a town or city — your browser is never asked for its location.

Chrome will ask permission to reach Open-Meteo — its popup opens at the top of the window, and Deny is the default. Choose Allow.

What gets sent

Open-Meteo needs no account and no API key. Archer sends the place name you type here to look up its coordinates, and then the coordinates to fetch a forecast — nothing else about you is sent.

Saved prompts

Type / in an empty search box to find one. Put {{blanks}} anywhere in the text and Archer selects the first one for you to type over — Tab moves to the next.

    Answers on the page

    With your own OpenAI Platform key, Archer can stream an answer straight onto the new tab instead of sending you somewhere. Everything else keeps working exactly as it does now.

    A Platform key from platform.openai.com — not your ChatGPT login. The two are separate accounts, and a Platform key cannot see your ChatGPT conversations.

    Where your key goes

    It stays on this device. It is kept in this browser's extension storage and sent only to api.openai.com, directly from the new tab page. There is no Archer server, nothing is proxied, and nothing is logged anywhere else. You are billed by OpenAI for what you use, on your own account.

    Model

    Listed from your own key, so this is what your account can actually reach rather than a list baked into the extension.

    Budget

    Answering stops once a day's total passes this. 0 means no limit.

    Why tokens rather than dollars

    Tokens are what the API reports exactly. A dollar figure needs a price list, and a price list hardcoded into an extension goes stale silently and in the wrong direction — so Archer only shows one if you enter your own rates.

    Permissions

    Archer asks for two things when you install it: permission to use your default search engine, and somewhere to keep your settings. Everything else on this list is off until you turn it on, and can be turned back off here.

    Turning one on is Chrome's decision, not Archer's: it opens a popup at the top of the window — some way from these buttons — with Deny focused by default. If a switch stays Off, that popup is where the answer went.

      What you ask

      Computed here, from the prompts already stored on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere to work this out.

      Your data

      On this device. Archer has no server, no account and no analytics — there is nowhere for your data to be sent, because nothing was built to receive it. Your prompts, pins, saved prompts, favorites and API key are in this browser's extension storage and nowhere else.

      What touches the network

      Three features, and only the ones you switch on: api.openai.com for inline answers, with your own key; open-meteo.com for the weather; and your own default search engine, through Chrome, when you search. Uninstalling deletes everything. The full policy.
